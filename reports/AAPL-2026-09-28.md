## Fair value and entry price

- Fair value = forward EPS $9.59 × fair P/E 30.8x, from the median year-end P/E of the last 4 fiscal years = **$295.06**.
- Band ±15%: $250.80 – $339.32.
- Margin of safety 15%: score 60.1 is at or above 60 (tiers: score ≥ 75 → 5%, score ≥ 60 → 15%, otherwise 20%).
- Entry price = $295.06 × (1 − 15%) = **$250.80**.
- Upside from the price $340.15 to fair value: -13.3%.

Stated limits (copied into every report):

- Banks and insurers are valued on book value (P/B), not earnings; this method is the wrong lens for them.
- REITs are valued on funds from operations (P/FFO); GAAP EPS understates them through depreciation.
- Cyclicals at peak earnings look cheap on a P/E exactly when they are most expensive.
- The historical median anchors to the past rate regime; a structurally different rate world shifts fair P/E.
- Consensus forward EPS can be stale or wrong; the fair value inherits that error one-for-one.
- Year-end P/E uses dividend-adjusted closes, which slightly understate older years' P/E.
- Pre-profit and near-zero-earnings companies (forward P/E > 100) get no fair value at all.

## Growth and quality

## Growth and Quality — AAPL
### Findings
- **Growth is not consistent across horizons.** Trailing revenue growth is 16.4% (growth.revenue_growth_yoy) against a three-year revenue CAGR of only 1.8% (growth.revenue_cagr_3y). The latest year is a sharp acceleration off a nearly flat three years, not a steady trend. The scorecard grades the two B and D respectively, and the growth category scores 60.0 (categories.growth.score).
- **Earnings show the same gap, with a buyback tilt.** Earnings growth is 28.7% (growth.earnings_growth_yoy) year over year and 27.1% (growth.earnings_quarterly_growth) for the latest quarter. The three-year figures are much lower: EPS CAGR 6.9% (growth.eps_cagr_3y) and net income CAGR 3.9% (growth.net_income_cagr_3y). Because EPS grew faster than net income over three years, per-share growth has been helped by a shrinking share count, not only by the business.
- **Margins are high.** Gross margin is 48.7% (profitability.gross_margin), operating margin 32.6% (profitability.operating_margin), net margin 27.6% (profitability.net_margin) and FCF margin 23.1% (profitability.fcf_margin). Profitability scores 95.0 (categories.profitability.score).
- **ROE is distorted and should not be read as the moat signal.** ROE is 148.8% (profitability.roe). That level reflects a thin equity base after years of capital return, not a return a new competitor could chase. ROA is 27.1% (profitability.roa), and that is the cleaner sign that the underlying economics are strong.
- **Ownership:** insiders hold 1.6% (ownership.insiders_pct) and institutions 66.3% (ownership.institutions_pct). That is a widely held mega-cap. Low insider ownership is normal at this size and is not a signal either way.

### Assessment
AAPL's quality is excellent. It earns high, cash-backed margins, with operating margin 32.6% (profitability.operating_margin), FCF margin 23.1% (profitability.fcf_margin) and ROA 27.1% (profitability.roa). The ROE of 148.8% (profitability.roe) overstates this because the equity base is small. Its growth durability is unproven. Revenue growth of 16.4% (growth.revenue_growth_yoy) and earnings growth of 28.7% (growth.earnings_growth_yoy) sit on top of a revenue CAGR of 1.8% (growth.revenue_cagr_3y) and an EPS CAGR of 6.9% (growth.eps_cagr_3y) over three years. A multi-year holder should treat the acceleration as a cycle upswing until more years confirm it. Quality supports resilience, but growth alone does not justify paying for a sustained double-digit rate.

### Risks and caveats
- **No margin history in the snapshot.** Only point-in-time margins are available, so this report cannot say whether margins are expanding or eroding.
- **Year-over-year vs. CAGR divergence.** If revenue growth of 16.4% (growth.revenue_growth_yoy) reverts toward the three-year trend of 1.8% (growth.revenue_cagr_3y), earnings growth expectations would compress.
- **ROE is not comparable to peers.** ROE of 148.8% (profitability.roe) is inflated by low book equity, and the scorecard's A grade on ROE partly rewards capital structure, not operations.
- **No breakdowns in the data.** The snapshot has no share-count, segment or geographic figures. The buyback reading is inferred only from EPS CAGR of 6.9% (growth.eps_cagr_3y) exceeding net income CAGR of 3.9% (growth.net_income_cagr_3y).
- **Ownership data is thin.** Only two holding percentages are available: no insider transaction flow and no institutional change data.

- Liquidity is thin on paper. The current ratio is 1.0x (health.current_ratio), so current assets only just cover current liabilities. The scorecard grades it C (metrics[health.current_ratio].grade).
- Cash generation is very large compared with the debt load. Operating cash flow is $146.7B (health.operating_cashflow) and free cash flow is $107.7B (health.free_cashflow). FCF margin is 23.1% (profitability.fcf_margin) on $466.8B of TTM revenue (profitability.revenue_ttm).

- Interest coverage is null (health.interest_coverage). The scorecard note says "no reported interest expense", so this metric was left out of the health category. The category scored 57.1 (categories.health.score) with only 0.7 coverage (categories.health.coverage).
- ROE is 148.8% (profitability.roe), much higher than ROA at 27.1% (profitability.roa). That gap reflects a slim equity base, most likely from capital returns, rather than a stretched debt load: D/E is still only 0.78x (health.debt_to_equity).

### Assessment
For a 1-5 year holder, the balance sheet is not the risk in this name. Apple has a modest net-debt position of $21.9B (health.net_debt). Against that it produces $107.7B of free cash flow (health.free_cashflow) and pays out only 12.0% of earnings (dividend.payout_ratio). The dividend is not under pressure, and nothing in the data points to a going-concern or refinancing problem. The weaker readings are a current ratio of only 1.0x (health.current_ratio) and a net-debt rather than net-cash stance. Both look like deliberate capital-allocation choices, not signs of distress, because operating cash flow of $146.7B (health.operating_cashflow) is well above total debt of $84.3B (health.total_debt). The scorecard's middling health score of 57.1 (categories.health.score) understates this strength.

### Risks and caveats
- Interest coverage is null (health.interest_coverage; also listed in meta.fields_missing), so debt-service capacity can't be checked directly. The scorecard note says there was "no reported interest expense". That is a data gap, not evidence that interest costs are zero.

- The snapshot has no maturity schedule, dividend-per-share history or buyback figures. So refinancing timing and total shareholder-return commitments (dividend plus buybacks) against free cash flow can't be assessed.
- ROE of 148.8% (profitability.roe) is inflated by the small equity base. It is not a clean measure of balance-sheet quality.
