Stated limits (copied into every report):

- Banks and insurers are valued on book value (P/B), not earnings; this method is the wrong lens for them.
- REITs are valued on funds from operations (P/FFO); GAAP EPS understates them through depreciation.
- Cyclicals at peak earnings look cheap on a P/E exactly when they are most expensive.
- The historical median anchors to the past rate regime; a structurally different rate world shifts fair P/E.
- Consensus forward EPS can be stale or wrong; the fair value inherits that error one-for-one.
- Year-end P/E uses dividend-adjusted closes, which slightly understate older years' P/E.
- Pre-profit and near-zero-earnings companies (forward P/E > 100) get no fair value at all.

### Assessment
By these numbers, NVDA's growth is both large and consistent. Year-over-year revenue growth is in line with the three-year CAGR, and earnings compound about twice as fast as sales, which shows operating leverage and not just volume. Margins are at levels that suggest pricing power, and ROE is backed by an equally striking ROA, so it is not a balance-sheet artifact. For a holder over one to five years, the quality question is not whether the business is excellent today. It is whether triple-digit growth and 66.2% operating margins (profitability.operating_margin) can last on a revenue base this large. The gap between FCF margin and net margin is the main item to reconcile before treating reported earnings as fully cash-backed.
